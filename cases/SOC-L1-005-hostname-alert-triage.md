## Executive Summary

The analyst reviewed an existing hostname-execution alert generated by a third-party Sigma-derived rule. The event showed Windows PowerShell launching `C:\WINDOWS\system32\HOSTNAME.EXE` under the operator's account. The operator confirmed running `hostname` to identify the VM during the instructed lab workflow.

The event matched the reviewed rule's behavioral conditions. Its authorized context supported closure without containment or escalation. The alert title alone did not establish malicious intent.

## Rule Review and Attribution

The analyst followed the rule-source link from LimaCharlie and reviewed `latest/windows_process_creation/proc_creation_win_hostname_execution.yml` in the `refractionPOINT/sigma-limacharlie` repository. The screenshot shows rule metadata crediting `frack113` and tags `attack.discovery` and `attack.t1082`.

The reviewed logic accepts `NEW_PROCESS` or `EXISTING_PROCESS`, requires Windows, and performs a case-insensitive executable-path suffix match for `\HOSTNAME.EXE`. A matching event generates the named report. It does not evaluate whether the user was authorized or whether the command was part of an attack.

This case documents investigation of an existing third-party rule, not authorship of a custom detection. The source screenshot is a record of the version viewed; current upstream content may change. No deployed rule export or byte-for-byte deployed/source comparison was obtained.

## L1 Decision

The operator explicitly confirmed that the command was executed as part of the guided VM-identification exercise. That explanation was consistent with the executable, command line, account, and parent process. Close the reviewed alert as benign authorized activity.

The broad behavioral rule detected its intended activity. Benign context does not by itself demonstrate a malfunctioning rule. Terminology such as false positive depends on the detection objective and the organization's classification conventions; this report uses an explicit benign-authorized disposition.

No Kill Process or Isolate action was necessary for this alert. The earlier LAB-003/LAB-004 response exercises were separately authorized tests and are not remediation evidence for this case.

## Scope, Confidence, and Escalation

The decision is supported for this event by telemetry and operator-confirmed purpose. The investigation did not review complete PowerShell ancestry, surrounding commands, other accounts, or all concurrent alerts. No claim is made that the entire endpoint is uncompromised.

Escalate if the user denies the activity, the parent/account/path differs from expected context, or related behavior suggests broader discovery or compromise. Include the event, available process identity, time-source limitations, rule logic, and unresolved questions.

## Continuous Improvement

Record the benign explanation rather than disabling the rule or broadly excluding hostname/PowerShell activity. Any proposed tuning should be narrowly justified, reviewed under the organization's process, and tested against relevant benign and suspicious contexts. No rule change or suppression was applied in this exercise.
